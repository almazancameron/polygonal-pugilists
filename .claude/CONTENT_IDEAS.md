# Content Ideas — Status & Technique Backlog

This is a running backlog of status/technique ideas from brainstorming (2026-08-31 and ongoing), not a commitment or a queue in priority order. Pull from it whenever authoring new content; update it as ideas get built, dropped, or refined. See `GAME_DESIGN.md` §3.2 for the underlying design lens these lean on: statuses as reusable pieces of delayed/recurring logic, not one-off special cases.

## Status ideas — very good / want to build

- **Stat-modifying statuses** (Fortify, Enlarge, Hone) — ✅ **Built.** Justified generalizing `modify_defense()` into `modify_stat()` as intended; Hone (a Power buff triggered on attack rather than on hit) also justified adding a new `on_attack()` hook alongside `on_hit()`.
- ~~**Vulnerability**~~ → **Ruin** (renamed during implementation) — ✅ **Built.** Increases damage taken by X%, applied after all other damage modifiers via a new `modify_incoming_damage()` hook.
- **Echo** — repeats the user's next technique. Not yet built — see the Echo architecture note below, since it's a different shape from every status so far.
- **Recharge** — ✅ **Built** (status side only — a stack that decays on its own turn by turn). The actual cooldown-gating behavior (a technique that applies Recharge to itself fizzling/weakening if the user is already Recharging) has no consumer wired up yet, but `StatusComparisonCondition` closes the tooling gap that made this awkward before — "self Recharge stacks < 1" is now a one-field condition, not a bespoke check. Just needs a technique to actually use it.
- **Infestation** — ✅ **Built.** Self-growing stack, gains 1 and deals 1 damage per tick; nothing yet reads or spends the stacks beyond that.
- **Ward** — ✅ **Built**, refined during implementation: blocks **both** positive and negative incoming status applications, not negative-only as originally scoped — clarified mid-build that "block both" was the intended answer to the design question the negative-only wording had left open. Intercepts inside `Combatant.add_status()` before the normal merge happens, as planned.
- **Thorns** — ✅ **Built.** Deals damage (scaled by stacks, losing 1 per hit) whenever the owner takes a hit.
- **Hex** — ✅ **Built.** Deals damage whenever any status is applied to the target, including Hex re-applying itself.
- **Absorption** — ✅ **Built.** A secondary HP pool; incoming hits drain Absorption before HP, unaffected by Defense.

## Status ideas — decent / worth exploring

- **Rage** — grows in stacks when hit; either a stat bonus directly, or a resource other abilities consume/read.
- **Expose** — temporary defense bypass, contrasted with Acid's persistent reduction.
- **Dodge** — consumable hit avoidance; should be harder to get or rarer than Defend.
- ~~**Persistence**~~ → **Stasis** (renamed) — **Up next.** Refined mechanic: whenever stacks would be removed from *any other* status, Stasis's own stacks are removed first instead. Architecturally the hardest idea here: every other status manages its own stack loss privately, so this needs a real cross-cutting redesign (every status's decrement path needs to check Stasis first, likely via a `Status.owner: Combatant` back-reference plus a custom `stacks` property setter), not just a new hook. Needs its own design conversation before implementation — flagged as possibly too strong combined with things like Dodge.
- **Retaliate** → **Retaliation** (built as-named) — ✅ **Built.** Deals damage back when the owner is hit, and that retaliation itself triggers on-hit effects — the one status allowed to trigger on-hits this way.
- **Technique-referencing statuses** — a general pattern for a technique to leave a specific, checkable mark rather than a generic status: "Marked by Snipe," "X technique on cooldown," "X technique category disabled." Needs a status that carries a reference to *which technique* applied it (a data payload, not just a stack count) — a different shape than every status built so far.

## Technique ideas — patterns to build from

- Big dumb hit. Big dumb hit with self-damage. Weaker hit + Defend.
- Bonus damage if the user defended last turn (or currently has the Defending status).
- Small hit + big flat heal (vs. percent-based healing).
- Multi-hit (or one big hit) that heals the *enemy* at the end (a deliberately bad-looking but potentially exploitable effect).
- Big frontloaded status applicator, for statuses worth stacking hard and immediately.
- Hit + status. Hit + multiple distinct statuses. Multi-hit + multi-status, especially statuses that interact with hits directly (Acid, Bleed).
- Pure multi-status application, for statuses with an on-reapply effect worth chaining (Burn's flare).
- Status consumption for a powerful payoff effect.
- **Conditional bonuses** — a priority-rule-shaped branch living *inside* a technique's own execution: "deal damage, apply Stagger, bonus hit if that capped into Stun." "2.0x power if target below 30% HP, otherwise 1.0x." "Bonus damage scaled by total enemy status stacks."
- **Battle-state manipulation as the payoff itself** — remove your own negative statuses, strip the enemy's positive ones, double the enemy's Poison stacks, give the enemy half your Acid stacks, swap/split HP values.
- **Temporary battle-state modification** — double Acid for one attack then revert at end of turn; reduce Foretell by 5 now but add 10 back at end of turn if it didn't pop.
- Effects entirely dependent on battle state — "heal for each Poison stack applied to the enemy."
- Effects that need "hidden" tracked state — total damage dealt/taken this fight, total statuses applied, total turns passed. None of this exists on `Combatant` today; worth building one small generic tracker once an idea using it actually gets picked up, rather than each idea inventing its own counter.
- Self-apply a negative status as a deliberate tradeoff — also a synergy vector for builds that care about having that status on themselves.
- Status "conversion" — turn stacks of one status into stacks of another.
- A technique that applies Acid but does massive bonus damage if the target has *no* Acid yet — a strong opener you later pay off by consuming the Acid you just placed.
- **Debuff techniques that get weaker against an already-debuffed target** — e.g. Recharge used offensively (a debuff idea discussed but not yet built): a hit that also applies Recharge to the target should deal less damage the more Recharge the target already has, so it can't just be spammed for free value. Already expressible today with no new engine work: `StackCountNumericBonus` (`count_on: TARGET`, `status_effect: RECHARGE`) with a **negative** `flat_bonus` — the existing "scale a bonus by a target's stack count" shape works identically for penalties, just with the sign flipped. Whether the debuff-application step runs before or after the hit in the step group decides whether the *just-applied* stacks count against that same hit (applied-before) or only pre-existing stacks do (applied-after) — an authoring choice, not a limitation.
- Grant the user an extra turn — a strong candidate for gating behind Recharge or a similar cooldown mechanic rather than being free.

## Architecture notes

- ~~**Self-targeting for status application**~~ — ✅ **Built.** `StatusApplicationAction`/`ModifyStatusAction` both carry a `Target { SELF, TARGET }` field; `TargetMissingStatusCondition`/`TargetStatusStacksBelowXCondition` later reused the same enum shape so priority rules can check either combatant too (see `DECISIONS.md`).
- ~~**Ward** needs an interception point inside `Combatant.add_status()`~~ — ✅ **Built**, as planned.
- **Echo** (repeat next technique) needs to reach into turn/technique-selection itself (`choose_technique()`/`take_turn()`), not just Status hooks — the first idea that changes *how a combatant decides what to do* rather than reacting to something that already happened. Not yet built.
- ~~**Stasis** (renamed from Persistence) needs every status's stack-loss path to check it first — a cross-cutting change, not an additive one.~~ — ✅ **Built**, via `Status.owner` + a custom `stacks` property setter, exactly as scoped.
- A composable per-action ordering inside `TechniqueStepGroup` (any sequence of hit/status-apply/status-modify/heal actions, not a fixed hit→status→heal order) — ✅ **Built**, motivated by a real limitation found while testing Enlarge (a technique needing to apply a status *before* its hit had no way to express that under the old fixed sequence).
- **Hidden battle-state tracking** (damage dealt/taken, statuses applied, turns passed) has no home on `Combatant` yet — build one generic tracker when the first idea needing it gets picked up, rather than piecemeal per-technique counters. Not yet built.
- ~~**`DamageBonus` → `NumericBonus` rename + expansion**~~ — ✅ **Built.** `StatusApplicationAction` wanting the same situational-bonus computation `HitAction` already had (flat + a percent term, optionally gated by a `Condition` or scaled by stack/status count) was a second concrete consumer of the identical shape — generalized `DamageBonus`/`ConditionalDamageBonus`/`StackCountDamageBonus`/`StatusCountDamageBonus` into `NumericBonus`/`ConditionalNumericBonus`/`StackCountNumericBonus`/`StatusCountNumericBonus`, files moved to `scripts/numeric_bonus/`. Bonuses still live on `TechniqueStepGroup` (`damage_bonuses` → `numeric_bonuses`), but the field ended up shaped differently than first planned: rather than a per-action `bonus_applied: bool` opt-in (dropped) *or* forcing differently-bonused actions into separate step groups (the first replacement plan — also dropped once a real counterexample showed up), `NumericBonus` itself carries `applies_to: HIT|STATUS|HEAL`, and `Technique._sum_bonuses()` filters a group's shared list by that tag before summing it into a given action. This matters concretely for "debuff techniques that get weaker against an already-debuffed target" (see the Technique ideas list) — a hit and a status application in the *same* group, sharing a repeat cycle, can each pull from independently-tagged bonuses in the one shared list without the hit's damage penalty leaking into the status's own stack count, which pure group-separation could not have expressed without splitting the group and losing the interleaving. The old `power_percent_bonus` field was later generalized further into `percent_bonus` + `percent_source: STAT|STATUS_STACKS|STATUS_COUNT|HP` (see below), rather than staying Power-only.
- ~~**`power_percent_bonus` → generalized `percent_bonus`**~~ — ✅ **Built.** Same prompt as the rename above, one level further: since the percent term already only ever read the user's Power, and a technique wanting "percent of a status's stacks" or "percent of HP" would've needed its own bespoke field, `percent_bonus` now pairs with `percent_source` (`STAT` — any `Familiar.Stat`, defaulting to `POWER` to preserve old behavior; `STATUS_STACKS` — one status's stacks or, via `percent_check_all`, every active status's stacks summed; `STATUS_COUNT` — how many distinct statuses are active; `HP`) and `percent_target: SELF|TARGET`. `ConditionalNumericBonus`/`StackCountNumericBonus`/`StatusCountNumericBonus` were also pulled onto a shared `base_amount()` on the base class instead of each re-deriving the flat+percent formula by hand, since duplicating this much more complex version three times over would have been a real maintenance problem.
- ~~**`StatNumericBonus`**~~ — considered, then dropped: once `percent_source` could already read any stat additively, a *multiplicative* raw-stat scaling (mirroring `StackCountNumericBonus`'s per-stack shape) didn't earn its keep — stacks are a natural "count of instances" to multiply by, a raw stat value isn't, and multiplying a whole flat+percent formula by e.g. a Power stat produces numbers that blow up fast for no clear benefit over the additive form.
- ~~**`StatComparisonNumericBonus`**~~ — ✅ **Built.** *Adds* the difference between two stats to `base_amount()` — e.g. "bonus damage equal to how much your Power exceeds their Defense" is just the defaults (`left: self/Power`, `right: target/Defense`). Same left/right/target/`compare_mode: STAT|FLAT_VALUE` vocabulary as `StatComparisonCondition`. Not clamped to a minimum of 0 — being behind subtracts from the bonus (a real penalty), not just contributing nothing.
- ~~**`StackComparisonNumericBonus`**~~ — ✅ **Built** (not originally planned, added once a concrete example came up). *Multiplies* `base_amount()` by the difference between two statuses' stack counts, e.g. "1 damage for each Poison stack you have more than the target" is just `flat_bonus=1` with the defaults (`left: self/Poison`, `right: target/Poison`). Multiplicative rather than additive like its stat counterpart, deliberately — "N *for each* stack of advantage" is a per-unit scaling, the same shape `StackCountNumericBonus` already uses for a single combatant's raw stack count, just with a difference standing in for the count. Same left/right/target/`compare_mode`/`check_all` vocabulary as `StatusComparisonCondition`.
  Neither built yet.
- ~~**`StatComparisonCondition`**~~ — ✅ **Built.** The `Condition`-side counterpart to the two bonuses above, for branching technique behavior on a stat comparison rather than scaling a bonus. Shares `StatComparisonNumericBonus`'s exact `compare_mode: STAT|FLAT_VALUE` shape rather than splitting flat-value comparisons into their own class — same "resolve left, resolve right, apply an operator" logic either way, only where the right value comes from differs (`right_stat`/`right_target` vs. a bare `right_value`), and keeping both classes' vocabulary identical means learning one teaches the other. Covers "does my Power beat their Defense," "does their Power beat my Defense," "whose Defense is higher," and threshold checks like "is my Focus ≥ 15" all as the same class with different field values — reusing `Combatant.effective_stat()` and the `Target{SELF,TARGET}` enum shape already used elsewhere.
- ~~**Two more `Condition` siblings, same shape as `StatComparisonCondition`.**~~ — ✅ **Built.** Not a merge into one mega-class — each resolves a different *kind* of fact, sharing only the left/right/target/comparator scaffolding, the same relationship `StatusApplicationAction`/`ModifyStatusAction` already have via the shared `Target{SELF,TARGET}` enum:
  - `HpComparisonCondition` — retired `SelfHPBelowXCondition`, which could only check the *user's* HP against a flat percentage. Compares current HP self/target/flat, plus a `use_percent: bool` (HP-as-percentage-of-max vs. raw HP — raw values are misleading once two combatants have different max HP).
  - `StatusComparisonCondition` — retired *both* `TargetMissingStatusCondition` and `TargetStatusStacksBelowXCondition`. "Missing" and "stacks < 1" are the same fact (a status still present always has `stacks > 0` — see `Status.is_expired()`/`_settle_status()`), so the presence check was always redundant with a stack-count comparison, not a genuinely different one. Compares a status's stacks self/target/flat, with independent `left_status_effect`/`right_status_effect` (cross-status comparisons like "my Poison vs their Acid" cost nothing extra once both sides are independent) and a shared `check_all: bool` (mirrors `StackCountDamageBonus`'s `count_all` — sum every status's stacks instead of one specific effect). Newly enables comparisons that weren't expressible before at all, like "more Poison stacks than your opponent," not just against a flat threshold.
  - Migration done: all three `resources/conditions/target_missing_*.tres`, `use_acid_bath_if_below_5.tres`, `use_acid_bath_if_missing.tres`, `poison_spammer.tres`, and four `resources/upgrades/*_upgrade.tres` files (`gorge`, `pure_restoration`, `concussive_blow`, `open_wounds` — found via a full-project reference sweep, not just the `resources/conditions/`/`priority_rules/` folders) all repointed and verified behaviorally identical to their pre-migration originals.
- **Nested-tooltip meta resolution only knows statuses.** `TooltipPanel._on_meta_hover_started()` (hover-linked tooltips-within-tooltips, e.g. Hex's description linking to Poison's) does exactly one lookup: `Status.from_id(meta)`. A technique upgrade's tooltip linking to a status already works today, but a status describing a *technique* by name, or a bare glossary concept like "on-hit" (not a `Status` or `Technique` at all — Retaliation's tooltip explaining what "on-hit" means would need this), wouldn't resolve to anything. Needs namespaced meta values (`[url=status:poison]` vs `[url=technique:venom_strike]` vs `[url=concept:on_hit]`) or a chain of resolvers tried in order — decide before authoring nested content that leans on more than statuses. Relevant once techniques themselves become hoverable (the planned priority-rule builder), not just technique upgrades. Not yet built.
