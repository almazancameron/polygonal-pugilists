## 2026-09-06 (session 9) — The tournament bracket: content tagging, a pre-fight flow rework, then the bracket itself

Three distinct pieces of work, in the order they happened.

**The reward system was silently running as uniform-random.** Investigating whether AI bracket opponents could reuse the player's reward machinery surfaced that **zero** of the 33 techniques, 17 passives, and 16 familiars had ever had `tags`/`role_*`/`species_affinities` authored — so `RewardSelector`'s whole weighted Species/Run/Pivot design had been collapsing to `BASE_WEIGHT` for every candidate since session 8, exactly the degenerate case its own doc comment predicted. Nobody had noticed because "tailored" and "random" look identical until you compare them. Tagged all 66 resources (Claude drafting, developer reviewing), then verified the fix rather than assuming it: a 2000-draw sample showed Ashwing's Species slot picking Burn-tagged Red Roc ~45% of the time against ~28% for an untagged technique, matching the intended weighting math. Two rounds of developer review followed, both catching real problems — first that species affinities leaned almost entirely on status tags and ignored the meta/pattern vocabulary, then that the whole `HEALING`/`BIG_HEAL`/`MULTI_HEAL`/`GETTING_HEALED` category was missing, leaving direct heals (Gnashing Maw, Fruiting Body's Infestation cash-out) with no vocabulary distinct from the Regeneration/Lifesteal/Renewal *statuses*. A self-audit alongside those caught `STAT_COMPARISON` applied to status-stack comparisons in three places (it should mean stat-vs-stat only) and a `SELF_STATUS` on Protean Purge, which applies no status at all.

**Pre-fight flow reworked twice on developer feedback.** A "Begin Combat" gate went in first, then the developer restructured it: the pre-fight screen now comes *before* the priority editor with two buttons (open the editor, or fight as-is), the editor returns to that screen rather than straight into the fight, and the "you haven't changed anything" confirmation moved off the editor's own Confirm button onto Begin Combat — because the case actually worth confirming is *skipping* the editor, and a technique picked but never dragged into a rule will never fire. That required `PriorityBuilder.has_unsaved_changes` to become public and `battle_controller.gd` to accumulate its own round-level edited flag, since the builder's own flag resets on every `setup()` and the screen can be reopened repeatedly in one round. A 1s pause before the first move also landed, so both HP bars are visible at full before anything moves.

**The bracket** (`GAME_DESIGN.md` §9), designed and spec'd first (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-06-tournament-bracket-design.md`), then built from a written plan in 12 tasks. `opponent_lineup` is gone; a real `Bracket`/`BracketRound`/`BracketMatch` tree replaces it, character select doubles as the bracket screen, and off-screen matches are simulated then rolled for upsets. Full architecture and reasoning is in `DECISIONS.md` — that's the durable reference, not this entry.

**§9.3's core assumption turned out to be wrong, in a useful way.** The design doc planned to approximate off-screen matches with a stat comparison, assuming real simulation would be too expensive. It isn't: a run needs at most 11 off-screen matches, and `balance_test.gd` had already measured a full headless battle at ~7.5ms. Running the real engine and reducing it to an HP margin — then *rolling* against that margin rather than taking the winner as final — is both cheaper and better, preserving the upsets §9.3 wanted without maintaining a second combat model that could drift from the real one.

**Things that went wrong, worth remembering:**

- **Re-saving `.tres` files through `ResourceSaver` drops their inline UIDs.** The tagging pass rewrote 66 resources that way; the editor then minted fresh UIDs and rewrote every file referencing them, producing churn across ~32 priority-rule files over the following hours. Harmless (references resolved by `path=` throughout, `--check-only` stayed clean), but the earlier in-session call that it was fully inert was incomplete — it self-healed *by* generating that churn.
- **A task boundary in the plan was wrong and had to be merged.** Tasks 6 and 7 (loop integration; off-screen resolution) can't ship separately: `advance_round()` requires every match in the round to have a winner, and only the resolver supplies the other seven. Writing a throwaway stub to prop up the split would have been waste, so they landed as one commit.
- **`click_node` fires a button's `pressed` signal regardless of visibility**, which repeatedly scrambled live testing — clicking a hidden `BeginCombatPanel` button mid-character-select popped the priorities dialog, and earlier clicking a hidden Restart button mid-fight started a second run while the first's coroutine was still alive, producing interleaved state that looked exactly like a bracket bug until traced. Two separate false alarms came from this before the pattern was recognised.
- **Forcing a win by zeroing HP and calling `check_victory()` manually double-advances the round**, because the natural turn loop's own call sees the same zeroed HP. Setting HP and letting the loop catch it is the reliable method.
- **The reveal summary was written to a hidden surface.** Off-screen results were logged at the exact moment `begin_reward_sequence()` hides the combat log. The developer's read was that a visible bracket makes log narration redundant entirely — so the lines were deleted rather than relocated, and the scouting screen now lists the previous round's results directly.

**Testing changed shape.** Rather than the usual write-then-delete `_verify_*.gd` throwaway, the bracket got a committed, re-runnable harness (`scripts/tools/bracket_test.gd`, 10 checks) — closing the regression-coverage gap session 5 flagged and never acted on. It uses per-check completion markers because a GDScript runtime error aborts only its own function, so a crashed check would otherwise report as a pass; that mechanism caught real crashes twice during this build rather than silently passing. The full 16-familiar round-robin was re-run at the end and matches its pre-bracket baseline, confirming combat itself was untouched.

## 2026-09-05 (session 8) — The real reward screen: weighted Species/Run/Pivot slots, then a long tooltip-reliability debugging arc

Built the reward screen §9.4 had been waiting on since session 6, then spent the rest of the session hardening the tooltip system it exposed real Godot reliability gaps in.

**Reward system, full architecture** (see `DECISIONS.md` for the durable shape): `RewardTag.Tag` (a closed, append-only enum) plus `TagAffinity` (weighted species affinity pairs) replace what could have been binary tag-intersection filtering. Each reward screen offers three slots — **Species** (weighted by the familiar's own `species_affinities`, static per species), **Run** (weighted by a `BuildSnapshot` computed from currently-equipped techniques/passives, favoring both repeated tags and under-invested roles), and **Pivot** (a "somewhat relevant, a little strange" wildcard, scored with an inverted-U curve that peaks at moderate build-overlap and *penalizes* heavy overlap rather than rewarding it) — via `RewardSelector.pick_candidate()`, a pure static-function module deliberately decoupled from `Combatant`/battle-state/UI so it's reusable later by AI drafting or simulation. `RewardProgression` owns the round-to-reward-kind cadence as an explicit authored array ending in a real `RewardKind.NONE`, not a modulo wraparound — rounds past the authored cadence are stat-upgrade-only, not a crash or a silent repeat. `RewardFlowController` (new `RefCounted`) orchestrates one reward-screen session: the RNG, the `BuildSnapshot`, reroll charges (3, shared across all 3 slots), and the stat-allocation state.

**Stat upgrades became allocate-then-confirm**, not immediate-apply-per-click, per explicit developer correction after reviewing `assets/ui_mockup/stat_mockup.png` — a new `StatUpgradePanel`/`StatUpgradeRow` (+/- buttons, live current→resulting preview, a Confirm button gated on every point being spent) replaced the plan's original "reuse the thin pre-fight button strip" approach, specifically to prevent an accidental click from permanently committing the wrong stat.

**Reordered per the developer's own reconsideration mid-implementation**: the reward-card screen now shows *first*; skipping it grants 2 stat points instead of showing the (now-redundant) single-stat screen twice. Passive-trade rounds still sacrifice first, then show replacement candidates, with skip only ever available on the sacrifice screen.

**Retired the pre-fight `PriorityBuild` picker** (the session-5 stopgap) — the player now just starts with their familiar's own authored default build; the real reward loop is what grows it from round 1. HUD elements irrelevant outside a fight (`Panels`, the speed-toggle button, the combat log) now hide for the whole reward sequence and reappear only once the next fight begins.

**Reorganized `resources/techniques/`**: 16 techniques that exist purely as an `OperationPassiveEffect`'s internal payload (never player-learnable) moved into their own `resources/techniques/passive_operations/` subfolder, flagged as a good idea back in the original reward-system plan and finally done — makes curating `technique_reward_pool` a matter of "what's in this folder" instead of a manual cross-reference check.

**The tooltip debugging arc** — reward cards need nested tooltips (hovering a status name mentioned in a reward's description) exactly like `TooltipPanel`'s own content already does, but nesting one inside a toggle-mode `Button`/`ButtonGroup` surfaced three real, compounding bugs, each requiring an actual root cause rather than a workaround:

1. **`TooltipLayer.hover_started()`/`hover_ended()` are keyed by source *Control*, not by the specific link.** One `RewardCard.description_label` hosts every link in its description, so once one link's tooltip is active, hovering a *different* link on the same card was silently ignored (`hover_started`'s "already showing" guard) — the old tooltip just sat there with stale text at a stale position. Fixed in both `reward_card.gd` and `tooltip_panel.gd` by tracking the last-hovered meta value per source and forcing the old tooltip closed before opening a new one when it actually changes.
2. **The viewport-boundary clamp read a stale size.** `_position_near_cursor()` read `panel.size` — a cached rect that only updates once Godot's own deferred container-layout pass runs, which one `await get_tree().process_frame` isn't reliably enough time for — instead of `panel.get_combined_minimum_size()`, which recomputes on demand. A stale, too-small size under-clamped the right/bottom flip logic specifically (the floor-clamp on left/top doesn't depend on size accuracy at all, which is why only two of the four edges ever showed the bug).
3. **`RichTextLabel.meta_hover_ended` just doesn't reliably fire for a link this deep inside a `Button`/`ButtonGroup` chain.** Confirmed by reading Godot's actual source (`rich_text_label.cpp`, `viewport.cpp`, `canvas_item.cpp`): the dispatch mechanism (`gui_find_control`, `_gui_call_input`) is provably Button-nesting-agnostic, and RichTextLabel's own meta-hover recompute is self-contained — in theory this should just work, and empirically doesn't. Also discovered along the way: `get_global_mouse_position()`/`get_local_mouse_position()` resolve through `Viewport::get_mouse_position()` → `DisplayServer::mouse_get_position()`, the *real* OS cursor, always — a synthetic `push_input()` event's own carried position is irrelevant to any code path that queries these (RichTextLabel's meta-hover included), which is why every attempt to test this mechanism via the MCP toolkit's `input_simulate` failed regardless of pixel precision. Worked around rather than fixed upstream: `TooltipLayer._catch_exited_active_tooltips()` now runs two checks — an always-correct "left the control's rect entirely" fallback, plus (for `RichTextLabel` sources only) a "drifted more than ~40px from where this hover began" heuristic, tuned down from an initial 90px after live feedback that felt too loose. Approximate by construction, not true per-glyph precision — the real fix, if ever pursued, is the developer's own suggested "give the link its own separate control" restructure.

**Priority-editor integration, later the same session**: `battle_controller.gd`'s `advance_to_priority_editor()` is no longer a placeholder — it shows the priority builder (now embedded in `battle.tscn` as a hidden sibling of the other reward-sequence panels), waits for confirmation, and writes the result back onto `player_familiar_data.priority_rules` before the next fight. The one real gap this required closing: the standalone prototype (session 5) had no way to reconstruct its block-tree UI from an already-authored `Array[PriorityRule]`, and no save/exit affordance at all — both had to be built. Reverse-population turned out cheap: `ConditionBlock.setup()` already read a condition's live field values for its widgets, so it only needed an optional `existing_condition` parameter instead of always minting a fresh one; a new `ConditionBlockDefinition.find_matching()` finds which of the 9 palette shapes a given condition came from via `(condition.get_script(), fixed_values)` as an unambiguous key — verified against every authored `.tres` in `resources/priority_rules/`, not just assumed, before writing any of it (confirmed all 16 familiars' conditions use exactly the three script types the palette already covers). A new `ConfirmButton`/`confirm_requested` signal gives the screen an actual exit. Verified via a headless round-trip script (rules → reconstructed segments → recompiled rules, asserted equal) and live via the godot-mcp-toolkit — reopening the editor mid-run correctly showed Guubal's real existing rules already populated, not a blank screen.

## 2026-09-04 to 2026-09-05 (session 7) — Five new familiars complete the 16-entrant roster, four real engine bugs found along the way, full-roster balance pass

Picked up mid-crisis (a live stack overflow in Relikarn, built just before this session) and closed out the 16-familiar roster: Mystbud, Shrumbus, Pebbloq, Berylazagor, and Ignimite all built this session, each following the balance-tuning workflow established here — developer describes a kit's theme/passive/techniques/priority rules, Claude picks concrete numbers/triggers and iterates against `scripts/tools/balance_test.gd` (the round-robin harness) until no familiar is doomed. See `DECISIONS.md` for the durable engine changes; this entry is what happened and why.

**Bug 1 — Retaliation self-hit stack overflow.** `RetaliationStatus.on_hit()` deliberately chains into `attacker.trigger_on_hit()` so a counterattack can trigger the attacker's own on-hit effects, but had no `if stacks <= 0: return ""` guard the way `ThornsStatus` already does. Relikarn's Grudge Strike needed a new `HitAction.target: Target{SELF,TARGET}` field (self-damage step), and a self-hit makes `attacker == target`, so the chain revisits the same not-yet-erased Retaliation object forever. **Bug 2 — Ward absorbed a combatant's own simultaneous self-applications.** Pebbloq's Chronoparry applies Defending, then Ward, then Stasis in one technique; `Combatant.add_status()`'s Ward-absorption check didn't distinguish "an opponent's debuff" from "a status I'm applying to myself in the same breath," so Ward ate its own teammate's stacks (and immediately expired itself) before the technique's cast even finished. **Bug 3 — a lethal hit could un-kill itself.** Discovered via a genuine stalemate: Regeneration's on-hit heal fires from *inside* `apply_hit()`'s own cascade (`target.trigger_on_hit(user)`), before `check_victory()` ever runs — so a hit that dropped HP to exactly 0 got healed back above 0 in the same resolution, making any Regeneration-holder unconditionally immune to death by direct hit regardless of how much overkill landed. Verified the fix's actual impact by running the full round-robin with and without it before committing (Berylazagor swung from a 92% outlier to a 21% one on the same numbers) — the fix (`apply_hit()` now checks `target.is_defeated()` before letting `trigger_on_hit()`/`DAMAGE_TAKEN` fire) was correct, but the developer had to independently retune Berylazagor's stats afterward since its whole kit had been unknowingly built on top of the exploit.

**Bug 4 — Cleanse's strip bypassed the passive system entirely.** `CleanseStatus.on_tick()` set `victim.stacks = 0` and erased it directly instead of routing through `Combatant._settle_status()` (the shared choke point every other stack-reduction path already uses), so STATUS_REDUCED/STATUS_REMOVED passives never fired from a Cleanse strip — silently blocking Ignimite's entire kit (Protean Purge is built around exactly that event). **New engine capability — `PassiveEffect.Trigger.HEALED`**, extended this session to fire from a status's own `on_hit()` heal (Regeneration) as well as `on_tick()` (Renewal) and a direct `HealAction`, giving Mystbud's and Berylazagor's "when healed, do X" passives full coverage regardless of which of Regeneration's two heal paths actually fired.

**Bug 2 — Ruin applied twice from one Ashwing turn** (real in-game report, diagnosed collaboratively — the developer supplied the two actual contributing sources and, after Claude's first two theories were wrong, corrected the mechanism to the right one: *"a turn should be that combatant's upkeep + execution phase"*). ## 2026-08-29 (session 2) — Status icon row, techniques-as-data, and the priority/build system (Steps 4–5)

**Techniques as data**: `Technique` (`Resource`, not `RefCounted` like `Status`) replaced four duplicated damage-formula call sites. First pass mirrored `Status`'s one-subclass-per-case pattern (`VenomStrikeTechnique`, `SearingSpitTechnique`, `AcidBathTechnique`, `AttackTechnique`), but the developer correctly challenged this: those three status-appliers were parametrically identical (same formula, different constants), not behaviorally different, so subclassing them was over-engineering. ## 2026-08-29 — Milestone 1 bootstrap + status effects (Poison, Burn, Acid)

Bootstrapped Polygonal Pugilists from an empty Godot 4.7 project into a playable 1v1 combat prototype, then built out the status-effect system.

**Combat skeleton**: `Familiar` (Resource) holds a familiar's build (stats, sprite) identically for player and enemy — no autoload, no asymmetry between sides. `Combatant` (RefCounted) wraps a `Familiar` with per-battle runtime state (current_hp, is_defending, statuses). `BattleController` drives a `Phase` enum state machine (PLAYER_TURN / ENEMY_UPKEEP / ENEMY_TURN / PLAYER_UPKEEP / BATTLE_OVER), resolves Attack/Defend, and ends the battle on victory/defeat. `CombatLog`/`CombatLogView` and `HPBar` are small, decoupled, reusable UI pieces.

**HP bar gained a damage-preview overlay**: `HPBar`'s root was restructured from a bare `ProgressBar` into a composite `Control` (`Bar` + `StatusPreview` + `Label`), so it can draw colored segments showing how much damage each active status will deal on its next tick, sized/positioned in pixels computed from the bar's current value.

**Status effects**: `Status` (RefCounted) base class, with `PoisonStatus` (stacking DoT, damage = stacks, decays by 1/tick), `BurnStatus` (fixed damage/tick, duration-based, "Flare" bonus damage + duration reset on reapplication), `AcidStatus` (stacking Defense reduction, 10%/stack up to 5 stacks, infinite duration). Upkeep became its own turn phase with its own pause, played only when the acting side actually has active statuses.

**Bugs found and fixed this session**:
- `check_victory()` never returned `true` — both branches fell through to the unconditional `return false`, since `get_tree().quit()` only requests a quit at end-of-frame rather than halting execution. Caused duplicate victory/defeat log lines and a doubled quit delay.
- A plain Attack didn't refresh the HP bar's status preview (only status-applying moves did), leaving the preview segment visually stuck at the old fill position. Fixed by consolidating every HP-changing call site through one `update_hp_display()` function.
- Two new-button omissions (Burn, then Acid): the button was wired to a handler but never added to `set_action_buttons_enabled()`, leaving it clickable during the enemy's turn.
- `apply_status()`'s combat-log source was hardcoded to PLAYER, which broke once the enemy started applying statuses to the player too (Guubal's acid attack) — fixed to key off which side is receiving the status.

**Enemy behavior**: Guubal's `enemy_turn()` now always uses an acid-based attack instead of a plain Attack — deliberately still zero decision-making (a single hardcoded action, not a coin-flip or priority list), to avoid quietly building Step 4's behavioral-priority system ahead of schedule.
